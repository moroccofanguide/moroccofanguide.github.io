Taxis in Morocco: petit taxis, grands taxis and ride-hailing apps explained

By the Morocco Fan Guide team · Published 17 October 2026 · 5 min read

In Morocco, taxis are the most practical way for a visitor to get around, and also where you are most easily overcharged if you don't know the rules. Petit taxis, grands taxis, ride-hailing apps: here is how it really works, and the habits that make you pay the right price, from someone who lives in Morocco.

Petit taxis: the meter rule

Petit taxis operate within cities. Their colour depends on the city: beige in Marrakech and red in Casablanca, for example. They are the simplest option for trips around town and for getting back in the evening.

The golden rule: insist on the meter and never ask for a price for the ride. A driver who refuses the meter and quotes you a flat fare will almost always overcharge you: get out and take the next one, there is always another.

  • Check that the meter is on when the car pulls away: at the start it only shows the flag-fall, just a few dirhams. If it starts higher or stays off, get out.
  • Carry small change: drivers don't always have change for a large note.
  • Note the taxi number shown on the car: useful if there is a problem or you leave something behind.

How much does a ride cost?

A petit taxi has no negotiated fare: it runs on the meter only. Keep an eye on it during the ride and check what it shows before you pay. As a guide, here is what a local pays in Marrakech:

TripMeter fare (daytime)
Marrakech train station to the Al Massira districtunder 20 DH
Long ride across town, around 4 km20 to 30 DH
More than 30 DHonly for an unusually long ride, beyond roughly 8 km
  • Night fare: the meter switches to a rate 50% higher, from 8 pm between October and April and from 9 pm between May and September, according to the French travel guide Routard. So the fare normally goes up in the evening.
  • The fare card: every taxi must display a laminated card on the dashboard with the rules and the per-kilometre rates, including the night rate. If in doubt, point to it.

A "cheap" price can still be a scam

Don't judge the fare by European standards. On the meter, a ride costs roughly ten times less than in Europe, because Moroccan purchasing power is nowhere near European levels: a baguette in a modern bakery costs between 1 and 2 DH, about €0.10 to €0.20. A flat fare that seems reasonable compared with London or Paris can be several times the real price. More benchmarks in our guide to prices in Morocco.

Grands taxis: fixed price per route

Grands taxis work differently: they connect cities or distant districts at a fixed price per route, often shared between several passengers. They usually leave once the car is full. If you want to leave straight away or travel more comfortably, you can pay for the remaining seats. Ask the price per seat at the taxi rank or at your accommodation before getting in.

Ride-hailing apps like inDrive

Ride-hailing apps such as inDrive are widely used in Morocco, including by locals, and many drivers are friendly. But be aware: carrying passengers without authorisation is prohibited, as the Ministry of the Interior reiterated in July 2025 (Finances News). The announced penalties target drivers, including possible vehicle seizure, but a police check can cut your ride short.

If you use it anyway:

  • check that the car and plate exactly match the app, and don't get in if they don't;
  • share your trip with someone you trust;
  • if in doubt, choose an official petit taxi.

On foot: mind the traffic

For a pedestrian, the most concrete danger is traffic. In big cities, many scooters ride fast and brush past people, even in medina lanes. Keep your distance, look both ways before crossing and step against the wall when a scooter comes through. And if a driver loses their temper, stay very calm and don't answer back. Frequently asked questions

Should I negotiate the fare of a petit taxi in Morocco?

No. Insist on the meter and never agree a flat fare: a price quoted in advance is almost always much higher. If the driver refuses the meter, take another taxi.

What is the difference between a petit taxi and a grand taxi?

A petit taxi runs on the meter within a city. A grand taxi connects cities or distant districts at a fixed price per route, often shared between several passengers.

Is inDrive legal in Morocco?

Carrying passengers without authorisation is prohibited, and the penalties target drivers. The app is still widely used; if you choose it, check that the car and plate match the app.

How much does a taxi cost in Morocco?

On the meter, a long ride of around 4 km in town costs 20 to 30 DH, and anything over 30 DH means an unusually long trip. In the evening, the night rate adds 50%. Trust the meter, not your European reference points.

This article reflects the experience of someone who lives in Morocco at the time of writing. Rules and practices can vary between cities and change over time.
