## Half-day trips

### The Agafay desert

About forty kilometres south-west of Marrakech, Agafay is a desert of stone and rolling hills with the Atlas as a backdrop. It is not the Sahara of the big dunes, but the atmosphere at sunset is striking. People come for a camel ride, a quad or buggy session, then dinner in a Berber tent. It is the easiest excursion to fit into a short stay. Find out more in our [Agafay desert guide](https://moroccofanguide.com/en/agafay-desert/).

### The Ourika valley

About an hour's drive away, the valley follows the Ourika river up to the village of Setti Fatma, where trails lead to a series of waterfalls. Mud-brick villages, terraced fields and riverside cafés make it a popular summer outing, as it is cooler than the city. Wear good shoes for the climb to the falls.

## Full-day trips

### Imlil and Toubkal National Park

Imlil is the starting point for hikes towards Toubkal, the highest peak in North Africa (4,167 m). Without aiming for the summit, one day is enough to walk between villages, explore the valley and have lunch with a local family. In winter, snow can cover the heights: check conditions before you go.

### The Ouzoud falls

At around 110 metres, these falls are among the most spectacular in Morocco. A path leads down to the pool, where small boats take you closer, and you will often meet Barbary macaques. It is a long drive: set off early to make the most of it.

### Essaouira

A complete change of mood on the Atlantic coast: ramparts facing the ocean, a lively fishing port, a UNESCO-listed medina and the wind that draws surfers. A same-day return is doable, but a night there lets you enjoy the town in peace once the day-trippers have left.

### Aït-Ben-Haddou and Ouarzazate

The ksar of Aït-Ben-Haddou, a World Heritage Site, is one of the country's most famous fortified villages and a frequent film location. The road crosses the High Atlas via the Tizi n'Tichka pass, at over 2,200 metres. As a day trip the return journey is very long: if you can, stay overnight or include it in a two- or three-day tour.

## The big sand dunes: 2 to 3 days

For real sand dunes you need to go much further. Zagora, about a day's drive away, can be done in two days but its dunes are modest. Merzouga and Erg Chebbi have Morocco's finest dunes, but the drive from Marrakech takes a full day: allow at least three days, with a night in a desert camp. Organised tours usually stop at Aït-Ben-Haddou, the Dadès valley and the Todra gorge.

**Tip:** if you only have one evening, the Agafay desert gives a great taste; if you dream of big dunes, set aside three days for Merzouga rather than an exhausting there-and-back.

## Booking your trip well

- **Group or private:** small-group trips are the cheapest; a private trip lets you choose your timing and stops.
- **Check what is included:** hotel or riad pick-up, guide, meals, activities (camel rides or quads are sometimes extra).
- **Read recent reviews** and favour free cancellation, useful if the mountain weather changes.
- **Pick the right season:** spring and autumn are ideal; in summer, choose Ourika or the Atlas by day and Agafay in the evening; in winter, desert nights are cold and the Tichka pass can be snowy.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best day trip from Marrakech?

For nature and cooler air, the Ourika valley or Imlil; for a change of scene, Essaouira; for desert scenery without a long drive, Agafay.

### Can you see the Sahara in a day from Marrakech?

Not the big dunes: Merzouga is a full day's drive away. For a single day, the Agafay desert is the closest alternative, but it is a stony desert.

### Should you book in advance?

In high season and during holidays, yes, especially for private trips and multi-day tours. The rest of the year, a few days ahead is usually enough.
